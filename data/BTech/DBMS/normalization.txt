Database Normalization

Normalization is the process of organizing relational database tables to reduce redundancy and prevent update anomalies. A relation is in First Normal Form (1NF) when every attribute contains atomic values and there are no repeating groups.

Second Normal Form (2NF) requires the relation to be in 1NF and every non-key attribute to depend on the whole candidate key. This matters especially when a table has a composite key. Partial dependencies should be moved into separate relations.

Third Normal Form (3NF) requires the relation to be in 2NF and contain no transitive dependency. A non-key attribute must not depend on another non-key attribute. Decomposing a relation into smaller related tables can improve consistency while preserving dependencies where possible.

Normalization reduces insertion, update, and deletion anomalies. Database designers balance normalization with query performance and may use controlled denormalization when read speed is more important than eliminating every redundancy.
